Grading

In total, students can receive up to 21 S/N/U scores and 13 participation points (see Coursework).

Final grades are determined by looking up a student’s counts in the table below. Rows list the grade a student may earn. Columns are the three participation tiers:

  • Full participation (11–13 points): eligible for any grade.
  • Partial participation (6–10 points): grades shift down one letter from the Full column.
  • Low participation (0–5 points): grades shift down another letter.

A cell of “n S + m N” means the student needs at least n S scores and (extra S beyond n + N scores) ≥ m. Extra S scores count toward the N requirement. Empty cells indicate that a grade is not attainable at that participation tier.

Grade Full (11–13) Partial (6–10) Low (0–5)
A 18 S + 3 N – –
A- 18 S + 0 N 18 S + 3 N –
B+ 15 S + 3 N 18 S + 0 N –
B 15 S + 0 N 15 S + 3 N 18 S + 3 N
B- 12 S + 3 N 15 S + 0 N 18 S + 0 N
C+ 12 S + 0 N 12 S + 3 N 15 S + 3 N
C 9 S + 3 N 12 S + 0 N 15 S + 0 N
C- 9 S + 0 N 9 S + 3 N 12 S + 3 N
D 6 S + 0 N 9 S + 0 N 12 S + 0 N
F else else else

Consider some examples:

  • A student with 12 participation points, 21 S, 0 N, and 0 U would get an A.
  • A student with 12 participation points, 18 S, 0 N, and 3 U would get an A-.
  • A student with 12 participation points, 15 S, 3 N, and 3 U would get a B+.
  • A student with 8 participation points, 18 S, 3 N, and 0 U would get an A- (Partial tier caps at A-).
  • A student with 3 participation points, 18 S, 3 N, and 0 U would get a B (Low tier caps at B).
  • A student with 12 participation points, 4 S, 0 N, and 17 U would get an F.

Resubmissions

The following work may be resubmitted one time each: assignments, the project Dataset Audit, and the project Written Proposal. On the other hand, Exercises, the project Defense, and the Project Repository may not be resubmitted. If a student receives a score of N or U on resubmittable work, they may resubmit that work one time for a score bump of up to one increment. In other words, a resubmission can raise an N to an S or raise a U to an N, but cannot raise a U to an S. Students should always make an earnest attempt to submit something relatively complete on time rather than missing deadlines.

How to resubmit? Students resubmitting work that at first earned an N should visit office hours prepared to present their revised work to the instructor or a TA and explain how they responded to feedback. Resubmissions that at first earned an N may not be submitted without visiting office hours. Students resubmitting work that at first earned a U should send their instructor a new submission via email, in the required file format(s), within the allotted timeline (see below).

Conditions on resubmitting:

  • Students wishing to resubmit must do so within three weeks of the original due date.
  • See the course schedule for the last day resubmissions will be accepted.
  • Students may not resubmit the same piece of work more than one time.
  • A score bump upon resubmission is subject to the same standards as the original round of grading, e.g., the expectation for an S is still high. Not all resubmissions are guaranteed a score bump.

Late policy

Deadlines in DATA 23700 are firm except under the specific circumstances below. They exist to pace the workload and give everyone a fair system of accountability. The grading scheme (with resubmissions) is designed to absorb a reasonable amount of sub-par work. Turning in something unpolished is much better than turning in nothing at all.

Exceptions to the no late work policy:

  • Resubmissions: Students may resubmit assignments, the Dataset Audit, and the Written Proposal once each, within three weeks of the original due date (see above). Resubmitted work can receive a score change of only one increment, i.e., U to N or N to S. Resubmission cannot be used for exercises, the Project Defense, or the Project Repository.
  • SDS Accommodations: Students with formal disability accommodations may be entitled to extra time on assignments.
  • Emergencies: If you have an emergency that makes it infeasible to complete coursework on time, contact your College advisor first — they should be aware of the situation and ensure any relevant university or department policies (e.g., SDS accommodations) are followed. Then email your instructor with your College advisor CC’d. Your instructor does not need the details of your emergency but needs your advisor to confirm that your situation qualifies. Reach out as early as is practical.

No other exceptions will be made.

Grade disputes

The score on a piece of work reflects the course staff’s feedback on a student’s level of mastery, and we ask students to treat it as an opportunity to understand areas for improvement. Students are encouraged to ask the course staff for concrete advice about how to improve their work — the instructor and TAs are happy to talk through your code or writing during office hours. Requests to change scores because a student feels their work deserved a higher score are not something we can accommodate, but conversations about how to earn a higher score on future work are always welcome.

There is one exception to the no grade disputes policy: if a grader made a factual mistake. Please note that this only includes cases where a grader makes an erroneous statement about your code or writing in their feedback. It does not include cases where students disagree with whether something deserves to be flagged as incorrect.

For example, suppose a student receives a piece of feedback that says “Poor choice of encoding for data type: Student used a part-to-whole representation for non-proportion data”. If the encoded data in question was actually proportion data, and the grader missed this fact (and erroneously gave you that feedback), you can ask the instructor or a TA to review this decision. Please note that, even if the feedback is amended, it may not affect the score assigned depending on how many other issues were identified in your work.

We ask that students keep these requests brief and to the point: no more than a few sentences identifying the exact statement that the grader made and the reasons the student believes the statement was mistaken, including references to specific parts of the submitted code or writing (e.g., “I said that these are proportion data in paragraph 2 of the submitted report.”). Focus on laying out the facts and nothing else. Regrade requests should be submitted through Gradescope, not via email.

Regrade requests are distinct from assignment resubmissions (see above).

Finally, it is also a student’s responsibility to make these requests in a timely manner. Requests to review grading mistakes must be submitted no later than one week after a graded piece of work is returned to you. We will not accept any request to review grading after Thursday December 11 because grades are due soon after; this may limit grade disputes for the last few exercises and the project.
